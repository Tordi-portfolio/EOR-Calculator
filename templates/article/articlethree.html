1. Fluid Storage — How Much Is There?

Volumetric OOIP (Oil) Foundation formula
N = (7758 × A × h × φ × (1 − Swi)) / Boi
SymbolDescriptionUnit
7758Conversion: bbl per acre-foot—
ADrainage areaacres
hNet pay thicknessft
φPorosityfraction
SwiInitial water saturationfraction
BoiInitial oil FVFres bbl/STB
NOriginal Oil in PlaceSTB

Every recovery calculation begins here. Always sanity-check OOIP from both volumetric and material balance methods. Calculate OOIP →

Volumetric OGIP (Gas) Gas reservoirs
G = (43,560 × A × h × φ × (1 − Swi)) / Bgi

43,560 converts acre-ft to ft³. Bgi is the initial gas formation volume factor in res ft³/SCF.

2. Porosity and Saturations

Archie's Equation — Water Saturation Petrophysics
Sw = [(a × Rw) / (φm × Rt)]1/n
SymbolDescription
aTortuosity factor (≈ 1)
RwResistivity of formation water (Ω·m)
mCementation exponent (≈ 2)
RtTrue formation resistivity (Ω·m)
nSaturation exponent (≈ 2)

The most fundamental petrophysical formula. Oil saturation follows as: So = 1 − Sw − Sg

3. Fluid Flow Equations

Darcy's Law — Linear Flow Foundation formula
q = (k × A × ΔP) / (μ × L)

Foundation of all reservoir flow calculations. Assumes laminar, single-phase, incompressible flow — know when these break down (turbulence near wellbore, multiphase flow, gas).

Darcy's Law — Radial Steady-State Well deliverability
q = (0.00708 × k × h × (Pe − Pwf)) / (μ × Bo × ln(re / rw))

Field units (md, ft, psi, cp, bbl/d). Used for well deliverability calculations.

Vogel's IPR Solution gas drive wells
q / qmax = 1 − 0.20 × (Pwf / Pr) − 0.80 × (Pwf / Pr)2

Use instead of linear Darcy for solution-gas-drive reservoirs. Pr = average reservoir pressure; qmax = absolute open flow potential.

4. Formation Volume Factors

Gas FVF PVT
Bg = 0.00504 × (z × T) / P    [res bbl/SCF]

T in °R (= °F + 460); P in psia; z = gas compressibility factor. Bg converts gas from surface to reservoir conditions.

5. Recovery Factor and Displacement Efficiency

Recovery Factor — Component Form EOR analysis
RF = Ev × Ed

Ev = volumetric sweep (fraction contacted); Ed = displacement efficiency (fraction displaced in contacted zone). Both must be optimised for meaningful EOR gains. Calculate RF →

Displacement Efficiency EOR analysis
Ed = (Soi − Sor) / Soi = (1 − Swi − Sor) / (1 − Swi)

Reducing residual oil saturation Sor is the primary objective of chemical and miscible EOR methods.

Mobility Ratio Waterflooding
M = (krw / μw) / (kro / μo)

When M > 1, water fingers through and bypasses oil — sweep efficiency drops. EOR (polymer flooding, etc.) aims to reduce M below 1. Calculate M →

6. Material Balance Equations

Solution Gas Drive MBE Drive mechanism
N = F / Eo
F = Np × [Bo + (Rp − Rs) × Bg] + Wp × Bw
Eo = (Bo − Boi) + (Rsi − Rs) × Bg

7. Pressure and Compressibility

Total Compressibility Transient analysis
ct = So × co + Sw × cw + Sg × cg + cf

Appears in almost every transient flow and material balance equation. Don't neglect cf in tight or overpressured reservoirs.

8. Well Productivity and Skin

Productivity Index Well performance
J = q / (Pr − Pwf)    [STB/d/psi]

Combines rock quality, fluid properties, and completion effectiveness into one number. Declining J over time signals damage or depletion.

9. EOR-Specific Formulas

Water Fractional Flow (Buckley-Leverett) Waterflood analysis
fw = 1 / [1 + (kro / krw) × (μw / μo)]

Fraction of the flowing stream that is water at any point. Foundation of waterflood analysis. Calculate fw →

Injection Rate at Breakthrough Timing
Qibt = 1 / (dfw / dSw)

Reciprocal of the fractional flow curve slope at the flood front — gives pore volumes injected at water breakthrough. Calculate Qibt →

Study Tips

01
Understand units before memorising formulas Many errors come from mixing field and SI units. Know which unit system each form of an equation uses.
02
Know what each term physically represents Bo isn't just a number — it tells you how much the oil shrinks when it reaches the surface. Physics makes the maths stick.
03
Practice on real data sets SPE comparative solution problems are publicly available and provide excellent real-reservoir data for material balance and flow calculations.
04
Learn to spot limiting assumptions Darcy's law assumes laminar flow and incompressible fluid. Know when those assumptions break down near the wellbore or in multiphase systems.
